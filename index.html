Four questions, and what a good answer sounds like


  

    

      “What is the out-the-door price?”
      
A good answer One number, said without hesitation, that
        includes tax and fees. They can write it down.

      
A bad answer A monthly payment. The payment is the wrapper
        everything else gets hidden inside — a longer term makes any price look
        affordable.

    

    

      “How many months?”
      
A good answer Sixty or fewer, and they will tell you what
        the payment is at sixty without being pushed.

      
A bad answer Seventy-two, eighty-four, or “whatever
        gets you comfortable.” That is a longer loan sold as a kindness.

    

    

      “What is on there besides the car?”
      
A good answer A short list with prices next to it, and no
        argument when one is removed.

      
A bad answer “It is a package” or “the
        lender requires it.” A lender requires insurance on the car. It does not require
        a service contract, paint sealant, key protection or a theft etch.

    

    

      “Are they giving you what your old car is worth?”
      
A good answer A trade figure they can compare to something,
        settled before the new car is discussed.

      
A bad answer A big trade number attached to a big price. The
        two move together, and only the difference is real.

    

  


  

    
What to say to them, not to the dealership

    
The thing most likely to go wrong here is not the deal. It is that they feel caught,
      get defensive, and sign to end the conversation.

    
✓

      
“Nothing has to happen today.”
      
Say it early. Every pressure technique in that building depends on today being
        special, and none of them survive somebody who is not in a hurry.

    
✓

      
“Send me the numbers, I’ll look while you get a coffee.”
      
It gives them a reason to leave the desk, which is often all that is needed. A
        salesperson cannot press somebody who is not sitting down.

    
~

      
Avoid “don’t let them rip you off.”
      
It puts them on the defensive about a decision they have half made. Ask for the
        sheet instead. The sheet argues better than either of you can.
